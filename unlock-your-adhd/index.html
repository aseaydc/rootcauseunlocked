The wider picture

What actually gets looked at

Not instead of your existing care. Underneath it. These are the factors the standard workup usually skips, and each one is testable.

Not one broken pathway

ADHD symptoms rarely come from a single system. The people who struggle most usually have several out of balance at once, which is answered by testing, not another strategy layered on top.

Diet removes before it adds

Refined sugar and high-glycemic carbs are hard on the ADHD brain, artificial dyes are linked to hyperactivity and impulsivity, and a supervised trial off gluten and dairy is often more informative than a food sensitivity test.

The gut-brain axis

The vagus nerve carries a constant stream of signal between gut and brain. Dysbiosis, overgrowth, or a compromised gut barrier create the kind of systemic inflammation that reaches the brain and impairs focus.

Heavy metals and toxic burden

Lead disrupts dopamine signaling and shows up at elevated levels in children with ADHD more often than in the general population. Mercury impairs methylation in ways that can look exactly like ADHD.

Nutrient status

Magnesium, zinc, and omega-3 status are among the most common gaps seen here. Zinc plays a direct role in dopamine metabolism, and the evidence is modest rather than decisive, which is exactly why it gets tested rather than assumed.

Don't guess, test

Most ADHD treatment decisions get made without a single biochemical marker: a questionnaire, a diagnosis, a prescription. Organic acids and nutrient panels look at what your neurotransmitter pathways are actually doing.

How the work is sequenced

Order matters more than intensity

People rarely stall on ADHD because they are not trying hard enough. They stall because the steps happened in the wrong order, or because a driver nobody tested for is still running in the background.

  • Testing before assuming. What your biochemistry is actually doing gets looked at before another strategy gets added on top.
  • Diet removals before additions. The highest-yield changes come from removing sugar spikes, dyes, and reactive foods before any supplement gets introduced.
  • Supplementation fills the gaps diet cannot close. Even a clean diet leaves gaps, especially where neurotransmitter demand is high and gut dysfunction limits absorption.
  • Progress is measured, not assumed. Symptom and function change over weeks are the scoreboard, and if they are flat, the plan changes.

On stimulant medication. Nothing here is a reason to stop, skip, or change a stimulant or any other ADHD prescription. Starting, adjusting, and stopping that medication belongs to you and the physician managing it. This work is designed to run alongside that care, not in place of it.

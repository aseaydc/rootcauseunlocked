Chemical Sensitivity

Unlock Your Chemical Sensitivity

A perfume aisle, a freshly cleaned floor, or new carpet can end your day, and people who have never felt it think you are imagining things. Chemical sensitivity is a real, recognized pattern with several genuinely treatable mechanisms underneath it, and it deserves a real evaluation, not a shrug.

Recognition

Does this sound like you

  • Perfume, a freshly cleaned floor, or new carpet can end your day.
  • People, including some clinicians, act like you are imagining it.
  • The triggers keep shifting, and no allergy test has ever explained why.
  • You have narrowed your world to avoid the places likely to set it off.
  • Nobody has ruled out the more common, more treatable explanations first, they have just told you to avoid triggers.

Multiple chemical sensitivity describes recurring symptoms across more than one organ system linked to low-level exposure to substances that would not cause acute injury in most people. It is a diagnosis of exclusion, which means other explanations get genuinely ruled out first, not dismissed in passing.

The wider picture

What actually gets looked at

Not instead of your existing care. Underneath it. These are the factors the standard workup usually skips, and each one is testable.

Not acute poisoning, not allergy either

A high-level exposure to a genuine toxin is a medical emergency, a different situation entirely. This pattern is recurring symptoms from multiple, unrelated substances at levels that do not cause acute injury in most people, and it is usually not explained by IgE allergy.

A diagnosis of exclusion

Before anything gets attributed to chemical sensitivity, other explanations get genuinely ruled out first, not just mentioned in passing. That is a safety step, not dismissal.

Sensory sensitization, and why it is treatable

Odors connect unusually directly to the brain’s memory, emotion, and threat systems. Once a smell gets strongly associated with feeling ill, the body can generate rapid, involuntary symptoms before conscious thought catches up. That circuit is genuinely treatable.

The autonomic and migraine overlap

Palpitations, dizziness, and temperature shifts are compatible with autonomic activation, which shows up alongside migraine, panic, and chronic stress. Treating the migraine can quiet the reaction even when other sensitivities remain.

Where mast cells fit, and where they do not

Mast cell activation can look similar from the outside, but symptom overlap alone does not prove it. A universal low-histamine diet is not assumed for every person with chemical sensitivity.

Building an evaluation that answers a question

A detailed history comes first: what was happening before symptoms began, which triggers repeat, what improves things. Testing gets ordered to confirm or exclude a specific possibility, never as a broad panel with no question behind it.

How the work is sequenced

Order matters more than intensity

People rarely stall here because they are not trying hard enough. They stall because triggers got avoided before the underlying pattern was actually mapped.

  • Red flags and other diagnoses are ruled out first, always. Treatable disease and real exposures get genuinely considered before anything is attributed to chemical sensitivity.
  • A structured trigger diary before any elimination plan. Exposure, timing, symptoms, and recovery get tracked, because memory alone misses the pattern.
  • Testing ordered to answer one specific question. Never a broad panel with no question behind it.
  • Autonomic and migraine overlap treated even when the trigger stays uncertain. That circuit is treatable on its own, independent of ever identifying every trigger.

Red flags come first. Difficulty breathing, blue lips, severe wheezing, throat swelling, or rapidly progressing hives need emergency care, not a protocol. Nothing here replaces that, and nothing here asks you to stop or change any medication your prescriber has you on.
